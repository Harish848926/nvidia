POTATO CROP AGRONOMY AND PATHOLOGY KNOWLEDGE BASE
Reference Manual for Potato Foliar and Tuber Disease Management

1. OVERVIEW OF POTATO (Solanum tuberosum)
Potato is a vital root and tuber staple grown in temperate, subtropical, and highland tropical regions.
Optimal tuberization occurs when soil temperatures remain between 15°C and 20°C.
Soil requirements: Well-aerated sandy loam with pH 5.2–6.5. Low soil pH helps suppress common scab.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. POTATO EARLY BLIGHT (Alternaria solani)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Pathogen Type: Fungal pathogen.
Biology & Spread: Overwinters as fungal mycelium and conidia in infected crop residue, infected tubers, and solanaceous weeds.
Wind and rain splash disseminate airborne spores. Disease pressure increases rapidly during alternating wet and dry weather cycles.

Visual Symptoms:
- Primary lesions appear on older, lower leaflets as small, brown-black circular or oval spots.
- Lesions expand up to 10–15 mm across, restricted by leaf veins, forming diagnostic concentric target rings.
- A yellow chlorotic border or halo surrounds the dark brown lesions.
- Infected foliage gradually blights, dries to a paper-like crisp, and falls off.
- Tubers can develop dark, sunken, circular lesions with raised purple-brown margins and dry, corky rot beneath the skin.

Severity Level: Moderate to High. Causes premature plant senescence and reduced tuber bulk size.

Organic Treatments:
- Apply liquid copper hydroxide or Bordeaux mixture every 7–10 days during tuber initiation.
- Spray horsetail (Equisetum) decoctions or biofungicides based on Bacillus pumilus or Trichoderma harzianum.
- Maintain adequate nitrogen and potassium; nutrient-stressed vines are significantly more susceptible to early blight.

Chemical Treatments:
- Protective contact fungicides: Chlorothalonil (Bravo 720) or Mancozeb (Dithane M-45).
- Translaminar & Systemic options: Azoxystrobin (Quadris), Boscalid (Endura), or Difenoconazole.
- Alternate FRAC modes of action (FRAC 7, 11, 29) to mitigate resistance development.

Preventive Agronomic Practices:
- Rotate crops with corn, small grains (barley, oats), or legumes on a 3-year minimum rotation.
- Avoid overhead irrigation late in the afternoon so foliage dries prior to nightfall.
- Destroy and till under potato vines and volunteer potato tubers post-harvest.

3. POTATO LATE BLIGHT (Phytophthora infestans)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Pathogen Type: Oomycete (water mold).
Biology & Spread: The infamous pathogen responsible for the Irish Potato Famine. Produces motile biflagellated zoospores.
Optimal infection conditions: temperatures between 10°C and 21°C combined with persistent leaf moisture or relative humidity >90%.

Visual Symptoms:
- Water-soaked, pale-green to greyish lesions rapidly expanding from leaflet tips or margins.
- Lesions turn into large, irregular, dark brown to jet-black necrotic areas.
- Under humid conditions, a fine white powdery down (sporulation) appears on the lower leaf surface bordering the dead tissue.
- Stem lesions appear as greasy, brittle, dark brown girdling cankers.
- Tubers develop shallow, irregular, sunken areas with brown to reddish granular decay extending into the flesh.

Severity Level: CRITICAL / Extremely Severe. Can destroy entire fields in less than a week.

Organic Treatments:
- Preventative sprays with copper fungicides before rainy spells.
- Rogue and destroy all infected foliage immediately upon detection.
- Flamers or mechanical vine desiccants to kill infected haulms 2 weeks prior to harvest to prevent tuber infection.

Chemical Treatments:
- Curative/Translaminar fungicides: Metalaxyl-M / Mefenoxam (Ridomil Gold MZ), Cymoxanil (Curzate 60DF), or Mandipropamid (Revus).
- Anti-sporulant fungicides: Fluazinam (Omega 500F) or Cyazofamid (Ranman).
- Never apply single-site systemic fungicides alone; always tank-mix with Mancozeb.

Preventive Agronomic Practices:
- Plant only certified disease-free seed tubers with blue label certification.
- Destroy cull piles, rock piles, and volunteer potato patches by burying under at least 2 feet of soil or treating with herbicide.
- Hilling up soil deeply around tuber rows protects developing potatoes from spore wash-off.

4. POTATO BLACK SCURF AND STEM CANKER (Rhizoctonia solani)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Pathogen Type: Soil-borne fungus.
Visual Symptoms:
- Stunted shoots, delayed emergence, and brown sunken lesions on underground sprouts and stolons.
- Aerial tubers forming in leaf axils.
- Irregular, hard, black sclerotia resembling "dirt that won't wash off" on harvested tuber skin.

Organic & Preventative Solutions:
- Plant seed in warm soils (>10°C) to accelerate emergence.
- Crop rotation with brassicas (mustard cover crops) that produce bio-fumigant glucosinolates.
- Seed tuber treatment with biological antagonist Trichoderma viride.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5. POTATO HEALTHY FOLIAGE CARE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Foliage Characteristics: Deep green compound leaves, vigorous upright stems, robust floral blossoms during tuber bulking, absence of yellowing or necrotic spotting.
Fertilization & Irrigation:
- Balanced basal fertilizer (e.g., 10-20-20 NPK) supplemented with foliar potassium and magnesium.
- Maintain consistent soil moisture at 65–75% field capacity during tuber set to avoid hollow heart and cracking.
