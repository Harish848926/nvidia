1. OVERVIEW OF RICE (Oryza sativa)
Rice is the primary dietary calorie source for more than half of the human population.
Grown under flooded lowland paddy or upland aerobic conditions.
Key growing requirements: high temperatures (20°C–35°C), high solar radiation during grain filling, and abundant water.
Common yield-limiting factors: fungal leaf blast, bacterial leaf blight, sheath blight, and brown spot.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. RICE BLAST (Magnaporthe oryzae / Pyricularia oryzae)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Pathogen Type: Ascomycete fungus.
Biology & Spread: Most destructive disease of cultivated rice globally. Can infect all aboveground plant parts (leaf, collar, node, neck panicle).
Spreads by wind-borne conidia under high humidity (>90%) and daytime temperatures around 25°C–28°C.
Exacerbated by excessive nitrogen fertilization and water stress in aerobic rice fields.

Visual Symptoms:
- Leaf Blast: Diamond-shaped or spindle/elliptical lesions with gray-whitish necrotic centers and dark reddish-brown borders.
- Yellow chlorotic halos often surround active expanding lesions.
- Individual spots coalesce, killing large sections of the leaf blade and causing a burnt scorched appearance ("blast").
- Collar Blast: Dark brown rot at the leaf sheath junction, causing the entire leaf blade to drop off.
- Neck Blast: Dark brown to black rot at the base of the panicle ("rotten neck"), causing empty whitehead grains and panicle breakage.

Severity Level: CRITICAL / Epidemic Potential. Can result in total crop failure within days.

Organic Treatments:
- Apply silicon fertilizers (calcium silicate slag or potassium silicate) at 200–500 kg/ha to strengthen epidermal silica cells against fungal penetration.
- Foliar sprays of bio-fungicides formulated with Pseudomonas fluorescens or Bacillus subtilis.
- Treat paddy water with neem cake extract.

Chemical Treatments:
- Systemic anti-blast fungicides: Tricyclazole 75% WP, Isoprothiolane 40% EC, or Kasugamycin 3% SL.
- Strobilurin/Azole combinations: Azoxystrobin + Difenoconazole or Trifloxystrobin + Tebuconazole (Nativo 75 WG).
- Optimal spray timing: Early tillering stage for leaf blast, and 5% heading stage for neck blast prevention.

Preventive Agronomic Practices:
- Plant blast-resistant rice cultivars with durable broad-spectrum resistance genes (e.g., Pi-ta, Pi-b, Pi-z).
- Avoid excessive urea applications; split nitrogen into 3–4 micro-doses instead of heavy single applications.
- Maintain continuous shallow flooding (3–5 cm) in lowland paddies, as flooded soil suppresses spore germination.

3. BACTERIAL LEAF BLIGHT (Xanthomonas oryzae pv. oryzae)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Pathogen Type: Bacterium.
Biology & Spread: Systemic vascular disease entering through hydathodes or wounds (often after typhoons or severe windstorms).
Favored by high temperature (25°C–34°C), relative humidity >70%, and standing water in flooded nurseries.

Visual Symptoms:
- Water-soaked to yellowish-green stripes initiating at leaflet margins near the tip.
- Stripes enlarge along veins, develop wavy margins, and turn milky-white to straw-yellow.
- Golden-yellow beads of bacterial slime (exudate) ooze from young lesions in the early morning dew.
- "Kresek" phase in seedlings: Rapid wilting, leaf roll, and death of whole hills within 1–3 weeks of transplanting.

Severity Level: High. Yield loss typically ranges from 20% to 50%.

Organic Treatments:
- Spray fresh cow dung slurry extract (5%) mixed with neem oil (folk prophylactic method with documented antagonistic microbes).
- Treat nursery seedlings with biocontrol strain Bacillus amyloliquefaciens.

Chemical Treatments:
- Copper oxychloride 50% WP mixed with Streptomycin sulfate + Tetracycline hydrochloride (Streptocycline at 6 g / 100 liters water).
- Spray Validamycin or Plantomycin at first onset of lesion elongation.

Preventive Agronomic Practices:
- Select varieties harboring bacterial blight resistance genes (Xa4, xa5, Xa21).
- Practice wide hill spacing (20 cm x 15 cm or 20 cm x 20 cm) for canopy aeration.
- Drain flooded paddy for 3–5 days (alternate wetting and drying) to suppress bacterial multiplication.

4. RICE BROWN SPOT (Bipolaris oryzae / Cochliobolus miyabeanus)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Pathogen Type: Fungal pathogen.
Biology & Spread: Chronic disease historically associated with the 1943 Great Bengal Famine. Typically indicates nutritional deficiency (potassium, silicon, manganese) or poor sandy soils.

Visual Symptoms:
- Numerous small, oval or circular dark brown sesame seed-like spots scattered across leaf blades and glumes.
- Mature spots have a grey to whitish center with a dark brown or reddish-brown margin and yellow halo.
- Infected grains become dark, discolored, and chalky, reducing milling quality.

Treatment & Management:
- Correct soil fertility: Apply muriate of potash (potassium) and farmyard manure.
- Seed dressing with Carbendazim + Mancozeb (Saaf) at 2 g/kg seed before sowing.
- Foliar sprays of Propiconazole 25% EC (Tilt) or Mancozeb.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5. RICE SHEATH BLIGHT (Rhizoctonia solani)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Pathogen Type: Soil and water-borne fungus.
Visual Symptoms:
- Oval or snake-skin shaped greenish-grey lesions on leaf sheaths near water line.
- Lesions spread upward to leaf blades, showing bleached centers with dark purplish-brown borders.
- Fungal web and small brown mustard-seed-like sclerotia form on affected sheaths.

Treatment & Management:
- Spray Validamycin 3% L or Hexaconazole 5% EC at early tillering.
- Deep summer plowing to bury sclerotia.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
6. RICE HEALTHY PADDY CARE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Foliage Characteristics: Upright, vibrant emerald-green leaves with uniform canopy height, strong tillers, and clean leaf sheaths.
Agronomic Practices:
- Apply balanced N-P-K (e.g., 120-60-40 kg/ha) with zinc sulfate (25 kg/ha) basal application.
- Implement System of Rice Intensification (SRI) with single seedling transplanting and intermittent irrigation to boost root oxygenation.
