1. OVERVIEW OF TOMATO (Solanum lycopersicum)
Tomato is one of the most widely cultivated solanaceous crops worldwide. It is highly sensitive
to fungal leaf blights, bacterial infections, and viral complexes, particularly in humid or overhead-irrigated environments.
Optimal growing conditions: daytime temperatures between 21°C and 29°C, well-draining loam soil with pH 6.2–6.8, and 6–8 hours of full sun.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. TOMATO EARLY BLIGHT (Alternaria solani / Alternaria linariae)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Pathogen Type: Fungal pathogen.
Biology & Spread: Overwinters on infected plant debris and solanaceous weeds. Spores spread via splashing water, wind, insects, and contaminated tools. Favored by warm temperatures (24°C–29°C) accompanied by heavy dews or frequent rains.

Visual Symptoms:
- Starts on older, lower foliage near the soil line.
- Produces dark brown to black circular or irregular lesions with characteristic concentric rings, creating a "target board" appearance.
- Yellow chlorotic halos develop around lesions.
- In severe infestations, foliage yellows, withers, and drops (defoliation), exposing fruit to sunscald.
- Sunken, dark, leathery lesions with concentric rings can also form at the stem-end of mature fruit.

Severity Level: Moderate to High. Can reduce yield by 30–50% if unmanaged.

Organic Treatments:
- Apply liquid copper octanoate (copper soap) or fixed copper fungicides every 7–10 days beginning at transplanting.
- Foliar sprays of biofungicides containing Bacillus subtilis (e.g., Serenade ASO) or Trichoderma harzianum.
- Apply neem seed oil (70% clarified hydrophobic extract) as a preventative surface barrier.
- Prune the bottom 12 to 18 inches of foliage once plants reach 3 feet tall to prevent soil splash.
- Apply 2–3 inches of clean organic straw mulch around the root zone.

Chemical Treatments:
- Protective contact fungicides: Chlorothalonil (e.g., Bravo Weather Stik) or Mancozeb applied every 7–10 days.
- Systemic / Translaminar rescue fungicides: Azoxystrobin (Quadris), Pyraclostrobin (Cabrio), or Boscalid (Endura).
- Resistance Management: Alternate FRAC group 11 (QoI strobilurins) with FRAC group 7 (SDHI) fungicides to prevent fungal resistance.

Preventive Agronomic Practices:
- Implement a 3-to-4-year crop rotation avoiding tomato, potato, eggplant, and pepper.
- Ensure wide plant spacing (24–36 inches apart) and stake or cage plants for maximum airflow.
- Use drip irrigation exclusively; avoid overhead sprinklers that keep foliage wet.

3. TOMATO LATE BLIGHT (Phytophthora infestans)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Pathogen Type: Oomycete (water mold).
Biology & Spread: Extremely aggressive and devastating pathogen. Can destroy an entire field within 3 to 6 days. Thrives in cool (15°C–22°C), wet, foggy, or humid weather (>90% relative humidity).

Visual Symptoms:
- Pale green to grayish, water-soaked, irregular lesions on leaves and stems.
- Lesions rapidly expand and turn purplish-brown to dark necrotic black.
- Under humid morning conditions, a delicate, frosty white fungal-like fuzz (sporangiophores) appears on leaf undersides.
- Petioles and stems collapse, emitting a foul, rotting odor.
- Fruits develop large, firm, greasy-looking bronze or olive-brown blotches.

Severity Level: CRITICAL / Extreme Emergency. Immediate intervention required.

Organic Treatments:
- Strict preventative copper hydroxide or Bordeaux mixture applications ahead of forecasted cool rain events.
- Once late blight is established, organic curative options are virtually non-existent; immediately bag and pull infected plants.
- Burn or deeply bury (>3 feet) infected vines; NEVER compost late blight material.

Chemical Treatments:
- Systemic targeted fungicides: Cyazofamid (Ranman - FRAC 21), Mandipropamid (Revus - FRAC 40), Fluopicolide (Presidio - FRAC 43), or Metalaxyl-M (Ridomil Gold - FRAC 4).
- Tank mix systemic agents with protective multi-site fungicides like Mancozeb or Chlorothalonil to prolong efficacy and curb resistance.

Preventive Agronomic Practices:
- Plant certified late blight-resistant cultivars (e.g., Mountain Magic, Defiant PHR, Plum Regal, Iron Lady).
- Monitor regional spore forecasting alerts (e.g., BlightWatch / USABlight).
- Eliminate volunteer potato and tomato plants and nightshade weeds near greenhouse perimeter.

4. TOMATO BACTERIAL SPOT (Xanthomonas spp.)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Pathogen Type: Bacterium (Xanthomonas perforans, X. euvesicatoria, X. vesicatoria, X. gardneri).
Biology & Spread: Seed-borne and survives on volunteer plants and crop residue. Disseminated by rain splash, overhead irrigation, and handling wet plants. Favored by warm (25°C–30°C) and rainy weather.

Visual Symptoms:
- Small (less than 3 mm), dark, water-soaked circular spots on foliage.
- Spots turn angular, blackish-brown, often surrounded by a distinct yellow ring.
- Centers of lesions may dry out and tear, creating a ragged "shot-hole" appearance.
- Foliage turns yellow, scorches, and defoliates prematurely.
- Fruit develops small, raised blister-like brown scabs that turn rough and cratered.

Severity Level: High. Significantly degrades fruit marketability and plant vigor.

Organic Treatments:
- Apply liquid fixed copper fungicides combined with bio-fungicides like Bacillus amyloliquefaciens.
- Spray actigard-compatible bio-stimulants that trigger systemic acquired resistance (SAR).
- Prune lower infected leaves only when vines are completely dry during sunny afternoons.

Chemical Treatments:
- Tank mixture of copper hydroxide and Mancozeb (Mancozeb enhances copper bioavailability, releasing more bactericidal copper ions).
- Apply Kasugamycin (Kasumin 2L) in commercial production where permitted.
- Bacteriophage treatments targeting specific Xanthomonas strains.

Preventive Agronomic Practices:
- Use certified pathogen-free seeds treated with hot water (50°C for 25 minutes) or 1.3% sodium hypochlorite.
- Sanitize pruning shears, stakes, cages, and seedling trays with 10% household bleach or quaternary ammonium.
- Strictly avoid handling, tying, or cultivating plants when foliage is wet.

5. TOMATO LEAF MOLD (Passalora fulva / Cladosporium fulvum)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Pathogen Type: Fungal pathogen.
Biology & Spread: Prevalent in greenhouses, poly-tunnels, and high tunnels with poor ventilation. Thrives at relative humidity >85% and temperatures of 21°C–24°C.

Visual Symptoms:
- Diffuse pale green to bright yellow spots on upper leaf surfaces with indistinct margins.
- A dense, velvety olive-green to grayish-purple fungal layer develops on the corresponding underside of the leaf.
- Lower leaves are infected first, turning brown, curling, and dropping prematurely.

Severity Level: Moderate in open field; High in greenhouse cultivation.

Organic Treatments:
- Potassium bicarbonate (Kaligreen) sprays applied at first sign of fuzzy growth.
- Copper octanoate or neem extract.
- Strip and destroy all lower leaves bearing fungal sporulation.

Chemical Treatments:
- Chlorothalonil, Mancozeb, or Difenoconazole (Inspire).
- Cyprodinil + Fludioxonil (Switch) for greenhouse high tunnels.

Preventive Agronomic Practices:
- Maintain greenhouse relative humidity strictly below 85% using exhaust fans and horizontal airflow fans.
- Heat greenhouse slightly at dawn to prevent condensation on leaf surfaces.
- Prune indeterminate sucker shoots regularly to maintain open canopy structure.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
6. TOMATO HEALTHY FOLIAGE CARE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Foliage Characteristics: Dark green, turgid serrated leaves, sturdy central stems, no lesions, yellowing, or deformities.
Nutritional Guidelines:
- Balanced nitrogen-phosphorus-potassium (NPK 5-10-10 or 8-16-16) to promote fruit setting over excessive vegetative growth.
- Ensure consistent soil moisture and calcium supplementation (gypsum or calcium nitrate) to prevent blossom-end rot.
- Apply mycorrhizal fungi and compost tea every 3 weeks for robust root immunity.
